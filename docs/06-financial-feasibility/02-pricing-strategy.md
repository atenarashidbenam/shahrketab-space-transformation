# Pricing Strategy

## Business Question

What pricing approach could support customer value while maintaining the long-term sustainability of the proposed Study & Work Space?

---

# Overview

Pricing plays a critical role in balancing customer affordability with business sustainability.

At this stage of the project, no pricing values are proposed because detailed financial information, competitor pricing, customer demand, and operating costs have not yet been validated.

Instead, this section identifies the principles, factors, and alternative pricing approaches that could guide future pricing decisions.

---

# Pricing Objectives

The proposed pricing strategy should aim to:

- Provide affordable access to customers
- Support long-term financial sustainability
- Encourage repeat visits
- Remain competitive within the local market
- Reflect the value provided by the service

---

# Factors Influencing Pricing

## Customer Factors

Pricing decisions should consider:

- Customer price sensitivity
- Visit frequency
- Perceived value
- Customer segment characteristics

---

## Business Factors

Pricing should also reflect:

- Operating expenses
- Facility maintenance costs
- Staffing requirements
- Revenue objectives

---

## Market Factors

External factors include:

- Competitor pricing
- Local economic conditions
- Inflation
- Customer purchasing power

---

# Potential Pricing Models

## Hourly Pricing

Customers pay based on the amount of time they use the workspace.

### Advantages

- Flexible
- Easy to understand
- Suitable for occasional users

### Limitations

- Revenue may fluctuate depending on occupancy.

---

## Daily Pass

Customers purchase access for a full day.

### Advantages

- Simple pricing structure
- Suitable for students and short-term visitors

### Limitations

- Less attractive for frequent users.

---

## Membership Model

Customers subscribe through weekly or monthly membership plans.

### Advantages

- Predictable revenue
- Encourages customer loyalty
- Supports long-term customer relationships

### Limitations

- Requires consistent customer demand.

---

## Hybrid Pricing

A combination of daily access and membership plans.

### Advantages

- Flexible for different customer segments
- Supports recurring revenue
- Offers greater customer choice

### Limitations

- More complex to administer than a single pricing model.

---

# Qualitative Comparison

| Pricing Model | Customer Flexibility | Revenue Stability | Operational Simplicity |
|---------------|----------------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Hourly Pricing | High | Medium | High |
| Daily Pass | Medium | Medium | High |
| Membership | Low | High | Medium |
| Hybrid Pricing | High | High | Medium |

---

# Initial Assessment

Based on this qualitative assessment, a hybrid pricing approach appears capable of balancing customer flexibility with revenue stability.

However, the final pricing model should only be selected after validating customer preferences, market conditions, competitor pricing, and financial feasibility.

# Pricing Principles

The proposed pricing strategy should follow these principles:

- Transparency
- Fairness
- Affordability
- Financial sustainability

# Pricing Assumptions

This analysis assumes that:

- Customers perceive value in the proposed service.
- Different customer segments have different pricing preferences.
- The service can remain competitive within the local market.

A detailed pricing decision would require:

- Competitor pricing information
- Customer willingness-to-pay research
- Estimated operating costs

- Revenue objectives

- Pricing should balance customer affordability and business sustainability.
- Different pricing models serve different customer needs.
- A qualitative comparison suggests that multiple pricing approaches are feasible.
- A final pricing model should be supported by market research and financial analysis rather than assumptions.
