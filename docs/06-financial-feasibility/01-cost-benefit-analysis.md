# Cost–Benefit Analysis

## Business Question

Is the proposed Study & Work Space financially justifiable based on its expected costs and anticipated business benefits?

---

# Overview

A Cost–Benefit Analysis provides an initial assessment of the expected costs and benefits associated with the proposed solution.

At this stage of the project, the analysis is qualitative because detailed financial information such as renovation costs, pricing, customer demand, and operating expenses is not yet available.

The objective is to identify the major cost categories, expected benefits, financial assumptions, and potential risks before conducting a detailed financial evaluation.

---

# Expected Costs

## Initial Costs

The implementation of the proposed Study & Work Space may require the following initial investments:

- Furniture and workstations
- Workspace equipment
- Wi-Fi infrastructure improvements
- Electrical upgrades (if required)
- Interior signage
- Initial marketing and promotion

---

## Operating Costs

Expected ongoing operating costs include:

- Internet service
- Utilities
- Cleaning services
- Facility maintenance
- Staff salaries
- Office and operational consumables

---

# Expected Benefits

## Financial Benefits

Potential financial benefits include:

- Additional service revenue
- Better utilization of existing gallery space
- Diversified revenue streams
- Increased repeat customer visits

---

## Non-Financial Benefits

Potential strategic benefits include:

- Improved customer satisfaction
- Stronger brand positioning
- Enhanced cultural identity
- Greater customer loyalty
- Attraction of new customer segments

---

# Cost–Benefit Summary

| Category | Expected Impact |
|----------|-----------------|
| Initial Investment | Medium |
| Operating Costs | Medium |
| Revenue Potential | Medium–High |
| Customer Value | High |
| Strategic Value | High |

---

# Financial Assumptions

This assessment is based on the following business assumptions:

- Customer demand exists for the proposed service.
- Customers are willing to pay for access to the Study & Work Space.
- The existing gallery can be adapted without major structural changes.
- Operating costs remain financially manageable.

These assumptions should be validated before implementation.

---

# Financial Risks

Potential risks affecting financial feasibility include:

- Lower-than-expected customer demand
- Higher-than-expected implementation costs
- Inflation and increasing operating expenses
- Ineffective pricing strategy
- Lower customer retention than expected

---

# Information Required for Detailed Financial Analysis

A quantitative financial analysis would require the following information:

- Estimated renovation costs
- Furniture and equipment costs
- Pricing model
- Expected occupancy rate
- Estimated monthly customer volume
- Operating expenses
- Expected monthly and annual revenue

---

# Preliminary Conclusion

The current qualitative assessment suggests that the proposed Study & Work Space has the potential to generate meaningful business and customer value.

However, a final investment decision should only be made after completing a detailed financial analysis supported by reliable cost estimates, customer demand data, and revenue projections.

---

# Key Insights

- The proposed solution may create both financial and strategic value.
- Several implementation costs must be assessed before investment.
- Financial feasibility depends on customer demand, pricing, and operational efficiency.
- Additional quantitative analysis is required before making a final investment decision.

---

# Traceability

This analysis supports the following project phases:

- Risk Assessment
- KPI Measurement
- Implementation Roadmap
- Final Recommendation

---

# Limitations

This Cost–Benefit Analysis is qualitative in nature.

No financial model, revenue forecast, or return on investment calculation has been performed due to the absence of validated financial data.

Future financial evaluation should incorporate actual implementation costs, operating expenses, customer demand, and pricing information.
